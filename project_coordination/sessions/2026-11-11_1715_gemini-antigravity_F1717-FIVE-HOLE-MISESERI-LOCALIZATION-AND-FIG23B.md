2. **Resolution of R2-F2 (Physical & Mathematical Scale Reconciliation: $10^{-12}$ vs. Physical $\text{MPa}$):**
   - In Abaqus, User Elements (UEL) cannot write built-in field contours or calculate SPR stress error indicators (`MISESERI`) directly. Researchers must overlay a companion layer of standard continuum elements (e.g. `CPE4` with UMAT).
   - To avoid adding artificial mechanical stiffness to the true UEL layer, the companion dummy modulus is scaled to near zero ($E_{\mathrm{dummy}} \approx 10^{-11}\,\text{kN/mm}^2 = 10^{-8}\,\text{MPa}$, as verified in `f42_mixed_uel_inf_stress.for` line 879: `EMOD = 1.D-11`).
   - Consequently, the recovered stress jump in the companion layer evaluates directly on the $10^{-12}$ scale.
   - In contrast, our native continuum pre-analysis evaluated SPR stress jumps on physical steel properties ($E = 210\,\text{GPa} = 210,000\,\text{MPa}$), yielding raw dimensional values in $\text{MPa}$ ($0.141$ to $367.15\,\text{MPa}$).
   - By the linearity of isotropic elasticity, $\mathbf{\sigma} \propto E u_0$. The relative error ratio:
     $$\eta_e = \frac{\text{MISESERI}}{\text{MISESAVG}} = \frac{E u_0 \widetilde{\text{MISESERI}}}{E u_0 \widetilde{\text{MISESAVG}}} = \frac{\widetilde{\text{MISESERI}}}{\widetilde{\text{MISESAVG}}}$$
     is **scale-invariant**: $E$ and $u_0$ cancel out identically. The sizing field $h_e = h_{\mathrm{old}} (\eta_{\mathrm{target}} / \eta_e)^{1/(p+1)}$ generated by Abaqus CAE is mathematically identical in both cases.
