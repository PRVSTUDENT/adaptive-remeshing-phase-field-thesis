### 3. Mathematical Analysis: Why Standard $L^2$ Projection Fails While $H^1$ Succeeds
In Bourdin/AT2 phase-field fracture, total fracture energy comprises both local potential and non-local gradient terms:
$$ \Psi_{\mathrm{frac}}(d) = \int_{\Omega} \left( \frac{G_c}{2\ell_0} d^2 + \frac{G_c \ell_0}{2} |\nabla d|^2 \right) d\Omega $$

1. **Standard Point Interpolation (Method A)**:
   - Interpolates nodal values directly: $d_i^{\mathrm{C5}} = d^{\mathrm{C4}}(X_i^{\mathrm{C5}})$.
   - While the local potential $E_{\mathrm{pot}}$ is accurately transferred ($9.154 \times 10^{-5}\text{ kN}\cdot\text{mm}$ vs donor $9.152 \times 10^{-5}\text{ kN}\cdot\text{mm}$, discrepancy $< 0.02\%$), the spatial gradient $|\nabla d|^2$ across nonmatching bilinear quad elements is attenuated from $4.379 \times 10^{-5}\text{ kN}\cdot\text{mm}$ down to $4.329 \times 10^{-5}\text{ kN}\cdot\text{mm}$ ($-1.14\%$ drop in gradient seminorm).
2. **Consistent $L^2$ Projection (Method B)**:
   - Orthogonal projection with respect to the $L^2$ inner product:
     $$ \int_{\Omega} v_h (d^{\mathrm{L2}} - d^{\mathrm{C4}}) d\Omega = 0 \quad \forall v_h \in V_h^{\mathrm{C5}} \implies M^{\mathrm{C5}} \mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{L2}} = \mathbf{f}_{L^2} $$
   - Minimizes $\|d^{\mathrm{L2}} - d^{\mathrm{C4}}\|_{L^2}^2$, but disregards gradient information. As a result, $E_{\mathrm{grad}}$ remains attenuated ($4.325 \times 10^{-5}\text{ kN}\cdot\text{mm}$), leaving total fracture energy at $0.134768\text{ mJ}$ ($-2.16\%$ vs Abaqus target).
3. **Natural Energy-Inner-Product ($H^1$) Projection (Method C)**:
   - Minimizes the actual fracture energy discrepancy by projecting under the natural bilinear form:
     $$ a(u, v) = \frac{G_c}{\ell_0} (u, v)_{L^2} + G_c \ell_0 (\nabla u, \nabla v)_{L^2} $$
     $$ a(v_h, d^{\mathrm{energy}}) = a(v_h, d^{\mathrm{C4}}) \quad \forall v_h \in V_h^{\mathrm{C5}} $$
   - Assembling $A_{\mathrm{energy}} = \frac{G_c}{\ell_0} M + G_c \ell_0 K^{\mathrm{grad}}$ and load vector $\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{energy}} = \frac{G_c}{\ell_0} \mathbf{f}_{L^2} + G_c \ell_0 \mathbf{f}^{\mathrm{grad}}$ restores $E_{\mathrm{grad}}$ to $4.387 \times 10^{-5}\text{ kN}\cdot\text{mm}$ ($+0.18\%$ of donor).
   - Achieves $E_{\mathrm{frac}} = 0.135602\text{ mJ}$ (**$-1.55\%$** vs Abaqus target, passing the $\le 2.0\%$ gate) and $RF2 = 114.88\text{ N}$ (**$+1.69\%$** vs Abaqus target, passing the $\le 5.0\%$ gate) simultaneously!
