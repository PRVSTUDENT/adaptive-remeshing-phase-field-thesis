## 5. Repository-Search Connection and Abaqus Solver Architecture Conclusion

### 5.1 Repository-Search Connection
In the thesis workflow, the Zienkiewicz-Zhu recovery-based error indicator (Abaqus MISESERI / element stress gradients) guides external mesh refinement. When the discretizations are non-conforming ($h = 2.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ to $h = 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$), field interpolation across steep damage bands produces high-frequency kinematic incompatibilities. While standard continuous fields interpolate smoothly, the degraded elastic stiffness factor $g(d) = (1 - d)^2 + 10^{-7}$ exhibits extreme sensitivity near $d = 1.0$, converting small spatial shifts into significant mechanical residuals.

### 5.2 Abaqus Repository Conclusion
Standard Abaqus/Standard Newton-Raphson solvers cannot traverse negative-definite tangent operators associated with localized damage softening in coupled user elements without continuation control. When the active-set boundary conditions release damaged crack-tip nodes, the localized softening snapback ($d$ advancing from $0.842$ to $0.895$) causes the standard static load incrementation to cut back repeatedly until $dt_{\min}$ is exhausted. Therefore, achieving robust Step 3 coupled re-equilibration requires either:
1. **Arc-length / Riks continuation** (`*Static, Riks`) to trace the non-monotonic snapback equilibrium path.
2. **Numerical viscous regularization** ($\eta \dot{d}$) in the phase-field evolution equation to regularize the local stiffness matrix during re-equilibration.
