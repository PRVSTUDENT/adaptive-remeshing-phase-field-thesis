\section{Supervisory Decisions and Methodological Limitations}
\label{sec:synthesis_limitations}

Following the 17 September 2026 supervisory meeting:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{71,320-Mesh Stiffness Defect}: Formally classified as \textbf{\texttt{RESOLVED\_AND\_CLOSED}}. The defect was conclusively proven to arise from the 16-entry keyword card limit in \texttt{*NSET}, silently dropping 134 boundary nodes. Multi-line card wrapping restored full structural stiffness ($K_0 = 137.820804\,\text{kN/mm}$, $-0.09\%$).
    \item \textbf{13,941 vs 71,320 Element-Count Reproduction}: Formally classified as:
    \begin{center}
      \textbf{\texttt{SUPERVISOR\_ACCEPTED\_REPRODUCTION\_LIMITATION\_CLOSED}}
    \end{center}
    The publication does not disclose sufficient details to reproduce the exact element count. Tested sensitivity trends ($1.0\% \to 71{,}320$; $2.0\% \to 17{,}687$; $3.0\% \to 8{,}120$; $5.0\% \to 4{,}356$) are preserved as permanent evidence.
    \item \textbf{Active Scope Holds and Gate 6B/6C Governance Dependency}: Mode-II, multi-crack configurations, and Gate 7 visualization remain paused. Gate 6B remains active pending the supervisor decision on whether the demonstrated endpoint energetic accounting plus the explicitly stated global-identity limitation (\textbf{\texttt{GLOBAL\_ENERGY\_IDENTITY --- NOT\_YET\_CLOSED}}) is sufficient for qualification; Gate 6C (State Transfer Energy) remains pending Gate 6B.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:synthesis_conclusion}

This thesis establishes that error-indicator-guided mesh refinement provides an automated, computationally efficient framework for phase-field fracture modeling in commercial finite element software. By leveraging Abaqus' built-in \texttt{MISESERI} recovery-based stress error indicator during a linear-elastic pre-analysis, non-uniform meshes achieve high local resolution in fracture process zones while reducing system dimensions. The rigorous identification of keyword preprocessor limits, disambiguation of structural versus material stiffness, decoupling of multi-quantity convergence metrics, implementation of non-invasive UEL energy logging with the Single-IP extraction rule, completed physical length-scale study, and formal demonstration of the mathematical incompatibility of a joint discrete potential for the implemented staggered displacement-damage coupling together establish a rigorous, reproducible scientific foundation for advanced phase-field fracture simulations.
