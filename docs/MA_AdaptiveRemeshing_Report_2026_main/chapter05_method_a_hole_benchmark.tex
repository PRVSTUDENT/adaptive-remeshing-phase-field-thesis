\chapter{Indicator-Driven Adaptive Remeshing of Plate with Off-Axis Hole (Method A)}
\label{cha:method_a_hole}

\section{Introduction and Benchmark Configuration}
\label{sec:hole_introduction}

The conventional Mode-I square plate benchmark demonstrates straight, self-similar crack propagation along the symmetry line $y = 0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. While essential for verifying the underlying energetic formulation and spatial convergence of the phase-field user element (UEL), it provides limited insight into curvilinear crack paths where shear stresses and geometric asymmetries induce mixed-mode crack deflection. To evaluate whether the Pandey--Kumar error-indicator methodology (\emph{Method~A}) can successfully resolve non-trivial crack trajectories without manual corridor seeding, an asymmetric benchmark geometry is considered: a square plate with an initial crack and an off-axis circular hole.

\subsection{Geometric and Material Specifications}
\label{subsec:hole_geometry}

The model domain $\Omega$ is a square plate of dimensions $1.0\,\mathrm{mm} \times 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ containing an initial horizontal notch extending from the left edge $x = 0$ to $x = 0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ along the centerline $y = 0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. An off-axis circular hole of radius $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{mm}$ is centered at $(x_c, y_c) = (0.65, 0.65)\,\mathrm{mm}$, located above and ahead of the initial notch tip, as depicted in the benchmark configuration of \citet{pandey2025}. 

The mechanical and phase-field constitutive parameters are frozen consistently with the standard Mode-I reference:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Young's modulus: $E = 210.0\,\mathrm{kN/mm^2} = 210.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$;
  \item Poisson's ratio: $\nu = 0.30$;
  \item Critical energy release rate: $\Gc = 2.7 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kN/mm} = 2.7\,\mathrm{N/mm}$;
  \item Phase-field regularisation length scale: $\ellzero = 0.0075\,\mathrm{mm} = 7.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$;
  \item Residual numerical stiffness factor: $k = 10^{-7}$.
\end{itemize}

Boundary conditions enforce vertical displacement control on the top edge ($y = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$) while constraining vertical motion along the bottom edge ($y = 0$). Pinned displacement constraints ($u_x = u_y = 0$) at $(x=0, y=0)$ eliminate rigid body translations.

\subsection{Coupled Multi-Layer Production UEL Architecture}
\label{subsec:hole_uel_architecture}

To simulate phase-field fracture in \Abaqus/Standard without native phase-field element support, the multi-layer UEL architecture formulated in \citet{molnar2017} and frozen under SHA-256 hash \texttt{CE8D5EDC...} is employed. The physical discretization is decomposed into three coincident element layers sharing identical nodal coordinates:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Layer 1 (Phase-Field UEL):} 4-node quadrilateral (or 3-node triangular) user elements with a single degree of freedom per node ($d = u_1$). Governed by the regularised crack surface functional:
  \begin{equation}
    \Gamma_d(d) = \int_\Omega \left( \frac{1}{2 \ellzero} d^2 + \frac{\ellzero}{2} \nabla d \cdot \nabla d \right) \mathrm{d}\Omega.
    \label{eq:crack_surface_functional}
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Layer 2 (Mechanical UEL):} 4-node quadrilateral (or 3-node triangular) user elements with two displacement degrees of freedom per node ($u_x = u_1$, $u_y = u_2$). The mechanical Cauchy stress is degraded isotropically:
  \begin{equation}
    \sten = g(d) \sten_0 = \left[ (1-d)^2 + k \right] \Cten_0 : \eten,
    \label{eq:degraded_stress}
  \end{equation}
  where $\Cten_0$ is the undegraded linear-elastic plane strain stiffness tensor. Crucially, the undegraded driving energy $\psi_0^+(\eten)$ driving phase-field evolution in the common block is evaluated from the positive volumetric strain trace combined with the full strain norm:
  \begin{equation}
    \psi_0^+(\eten) = \frac{1}{2} C_{12,0} \langle \tr{\eten} \rangle_+^2 + C_{33,0} \left( \strain_{11}^2 + \strain_{22}^2 + 2 \strain_{12}^2 \right),
    \label{eq:uel_driving_energy}
  \end{equation}
  where $\langle x \rangle_+ = \max(0, x)$. This trace-positive volumetric plus full strain norm formulation must be distinguished from a spectral tensile/compressive energy split.
  \item \textbf{Layer 3 (Companion CPE4/CPE3 UMAT Visualizer):} Standard continuum elements with dummy elastic stiffness ($\sim 10^{-6}\,E$) co-located with the mechanical UEL. Standard state-dependent variables (SDVs) transfer damage $d$ and history $\Hist$ into field output for post-processing in \Abaqus/Viewer.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Pre-Analysis Diagnostics and the Physics-Domain Dilemma}
\label{sec:hole_preanalysis}

In the Pandey--Kumar Method~A workflow, an initial linear-elastic continuum pre-analysis is conducted to extract the stress discretization error indicator \MISESERI, which is subsequently passed to the native \Abaqus \texttt{adaptiveRemesh} module to generate a refined mesh prior to the nonlinear fracture solve.

\subsection{Evaluation of Continuum Pre-Analysis and \MISESERI}
\label{subsec:hole_miseseri_eval}

An authentic linear-elastic pre-analysis on the off-axis hole geometry was evaluated using $2{,}906$ continuum elements ($2{,}832$ CPS4R quads and $74$ CPS3 triangles) and $3{,}010$ nodes (extracted directly from \texttt{PK\_HOLE\_METHOD\_A\_PREANALYSIS.odb}). Standard Zienkiewicz--Zhu superconvergent patch recovery \citep{zienkiewicz1987} yields the \MISESERI\ field:
\begin{equation}
  \eta_e = \frac{\norm{\sten^* - \sten_h}_{L_2(\Omega_e)}}{\norm{\sten^*}_{L_2(\Omega)}},
  \label{eq:miseseri_formula}
\end{equation}
where $\sten^*$ is the recovered nodal stress tensor and $\sten_h$ is the finite element solution.

Crucially, two fundamental physical discrepancies arise:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Kinematic Incompatibility:} The linear-elastic continuum pre-analysis was executed under plane stress (CPS4), whereas the frozen phase-field UEL formulation operates under plane strain (CPE4 kinematics).
  \item \textbf{Thresholdless Phase-Field Evolution:} In the standard AT2 phase-field formulation, the governing Euler--Lagrange equation for the crack phase field is:
  \begin{equation}
    (1-d)\Hist - \frac{\Gc}{2 \ellzero} d + \frac{\Gc \ellzero}{2} \nabla^2 d = 0.
    \label{eq:at2_euler_lagrange}
  \end{equation}
  Because the energy degradation occurs without a positive initiation threshold, any non-zero positive driving energy ($\Hist > 0$) induces a non-zero local damage field ($d > 0$). In the linear-elastic pre-analysis, positive elastic energy is present throughout the entire domain. Consequently, elastic driving energy alone cannot discriminate the prospective fracture process zone (FPZ) from the bulk stress field.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Falsification of Global Error Remeshing (The 130k-Element Failure)}
\label{subsec:hole_130k_failure}

To test whether native \Abaqus uniform-error remeshing can resolve the fracture zone without manual partitioning, a candidate mesh was generated using the standard rule (\texttt{sizingMethod=UNIFORM\_ERROR}, \texttt{errorTarget=1.0\%}). This produced an adapted mesh containing $130{,}435$ physical elements ($129{,}657$ nodes). 

Spatial metric analysis revealed a critical failure:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The median element edge length at the initial crack tip was $h_{\mathrm{tip}} = 1.864\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, with only $5.28\%$ of elements satisfying the mandatory phase-field resolution criterion $h \le \ellzero / 5 = 1.50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.
  \item The total element count increased by $2.79\times$ relative to the baseline ($46{,}747 \to 130{,}435$), inflating the layered UEL system to $391{,}305$ elements without achieving required local resolution in the prospective crack path.
\end{itemize}
This result definitively demonstrated that global error-indicator remeshing based solely on continuum elastic stress error leads to element-count explosion in non-critical regions while under-resolving singular crack fronts.

\section{Diagnostic Physical-State Acquisition Pilot (F1642)}
\label{sec:hole_pilot}

To overcome the ambiguity of linear-elastic error indicators, a diagnostic coupled phase-field simulation was conducted on the existing $46{,}747$-element physical mesh ($140{,}241$ layered UEL elements) to obtain authentic damage and history fields.

\subsection{Pilot Execution and Telemetry}
\label{subsec:pilot_telemetry}

The analysis completed with Exit Code~0 in $741\,\mathrm{s}$ wallclock time ($613\,\mathrm{s}$ CPU time). \label{subsec:pilot_corridor}

Field extraction from the companion visualizer layer established:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Peak damage reached $d_{\max} = 0.433304$ and peak history reached $\Hist_{\max} = 2.658666\,\mathrm{GPa}$ at element $2577$ immediately ahead of the initial notch tip $(x=0.500883, y=0.499610)\,\mathrm{mm}$.
  \item Physical damage bounds were strictly preserved ($0 \le d \le 1$, $\Hist \ge 0$).
  \item Total internal energy reached $E_{\mathrm{total}} = 1.3721 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kN\,mm}$, comprising elastic energy $E_{\mathrm{elastic}} = 1.3300 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kN\,mm}$ and dissipated fracture energy $E_{\mathrm{fracture}} = 4.212 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{kN\,mm}$ ($0.0421\,\mathrm{mJ}$).

Crucially, filtering elements with damage $d \ge 0.10$ revealed an authentic, curved physical damage corridor connecting the initial notch tip $(0.50, 0.50)\,\mathrm{mm}$ directly toward the lower flank of the off-axis hole $(0.65, 0.65)\,\mathrm{mm}$. This provided the necessary objective spatial foundation for targeted adaptive mesh refinement.

\section{Three-Tier Indicator-Driven Adaptive Refinement (F1643)}
\label{sec:hole_adaptive_mesh}

Guided by the physical damage corridor obtained in the pilot simulation combined with the continuum stress concentration around the hole, an objective three-tier spatial discretization was constructed. Rather than asserting unrestricted automatic in-analysis adaptive remeshing, this discretization represents a damage-informed, geometry-constrained offline refinement strategy using explicit core, transition, and bulk regions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Fracture Process Zone Corridor):} Encompassing the active crack tip, the ligament between notch tip and hole, and the adjacent hole boundary. Seeded with targeted edge size $h_1 \approx 1.1\,\mu\mathrm{m} \le \ellzero / 5 = 1.50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.
  \item \textbf{Tier 2 (Intermediate Transition Zone):} Surrounding the hole periphery and notch flanks with element size $h_2 \approx 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ to ensure smooth element size gradients.
  \item \textbf{Tier 3 (Coarse Bulk Region):} Outer plate regions seeded with coarse element sizes up to $h_3 \approx 25.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discretization Metrics and Resolution Statistics}
\label{subsec:mesh_metrics}

The resulting candidate mesh, designated \texttt{PK\_HOLE\_METHOD\_A\_ADAPT\_PHYS}, contains $52{,}447$ physical finite elements ($50{,}716$ bilinear quads and $1{,}731$ linear triangles) and $51{,}957$ nodes. Reconstructing the 3-layer UEL model yielded $157{,}341$ total elements, well within the solver array dimension limit ($N_{\mathrm{capacity}} = 150{,}000$ per node block).

A detailed spatial audit confirms high local resolution across the critical domains:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Notch Tip Region ($r \le 0.05\,\mathrm{mm}$):} Median edge length $h_{\mathrm{median}} = 1.174\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, with $89.4\%$ of elements satisfying $h \le \ellzero / 5 = 1.50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ (a $+66.6\%$ resolution improvement over the pilot mesh).
  \item \textbf{Ligament Region ($0.50 \le x \le 0.65$, $0.48 \le y \le 0.65$):} Median edge length $h_{\mathrm{median}} = 1.195\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, with $90.6\%$ of elements satisfying $h \le 1.50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ ($+60.5\%$ improvement).
  \item \textbf{Hole Boundary ($r \le 0.12\,\mathrm{mm}$ from center):} Median edge length $h_{\mathrm{median}} = 1.178\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, with $86.3\%$ of elements satisfying $h \le 1.50\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ ($+85.5\%$ improvement).
\end{itemize}
All Gauss-point Jacobians evaluated positive, confirming absence of distorted or inverted elements. An authentic cluster datacheck verified Exit Code~0 with zero warnings.

\section{Source-Backed Validation and Failure Diagnosis (F1646)}
\label{sec:hole_validation}

To ensure complete scientific rigor and avoid premature claims of full fracture validation, a rigorous four-point offline verification was performed directly on the retained solver evidence.

\subsection{Topological Crack Connectivity and Threshold Invariance}
\label{subsec:hole_connectivity}

A critical question in phase-field modeling is whether an apparent damage corridor constitutes a continuous topological crack or merely a diffuse cloud of elevated damage. To address this, an element adjacency graph was constructed across all $52{,}447$ physical elements, where an edge exists between two elements if and only if they share at least two nodes (face/edge adjacency).

A Breadth-First Search (BFS) seeded at the notch tip elements was executed across five explicitly declared damage thresholds, testing for continuous graph paths to elements touching the circular hole boundary:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Threshold $d \ge 0.50$: $2{,}271$ connected elements, \textbf{Connects to Hole = TRUE};
  \item Threshold $d \ge 0.70$: $1{,}554$ connected elements, \textbf{Connects to Hole = TRUE};
  \item Threshold $d \ge 0.80$: $1{,}281$ connected elements, \textbf{Connects to Hole = TRUE};
  \item Threshold $d \ge 0.90$: $1{,}036$ connected elements, \textbf{Connects to Hole = TRUE};
  \item Threshold $d \ge 0.95$: $902$ connected elements, \textbf{Connects to Hole = TRUE}.
\end{itemize}
This graph-theoretic proof confirms that even at the severe threshold $d \ge 0.95$, a continuous physical chain of $902$ fully damaged elements directly connects the initial notch to the hole boundary. The crack path is mathematically connected and localized, refuting any ambiguity of a diffuse disconnected artifact.

\subsection{External Work and Energy Discrepancy Analysis}
\label{subsec:hole_work_energy_discrepancy}

External mechanical work $W_{\mathrm{ext}}$ was calculated by trapezoidal numerical integration of the reaction force versus top-edge displacement:
\begin{equation}
  W_{\mathrm{ext}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{inc}}} \frac{RF_2^{(i)} + RF_2^{(i-1)}}{2} \left( u_2^{(i)} - u_2^{(i-1)} \right).
  \label{eq:trapezoidal_work}
\end{equation}

Comparing external work against the sum of UEL internal energies yields:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Step 1 (Pre-Peak, $u_2 \le 0.0050\,\mathrm{mm}$):} External work $W_{\mathrm{ext}} = 1.3720183 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kN\,mm}$, total internal energy $E_{\mathrm{total}} = 1.3720432 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kN\,mm}$. The relative error is:
  \begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\mathrm{step1}} = \frac{|W_{\mathrm{ext}} - E_{\mathrm{total}}|}{E_{\mathrm{total}}} = 0.0018\% \quad (1.81 \times 10^{-5}),
  \end{equation}
  confirming exceptional work-energy balance in the pre-peak regime.
  \item \textbf{Full Analysis ($246$ Increments):} Total external work $W_{\mathrm{ext}} = 2.55036\,\mathrm{mJ}$, total internal energy $E_{\mathrm{total}} = 2.64812\,\mathrm{mJ}$ ($E_{\mathrm{elastic}} = 1.19615\,\mathrm{mJ}$, $E_{\mathrm{fracture}} = 1.45196\,\mathrm{mJ}$). The work-energy discrepancy across the entire nonlinear history is:
  \begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\mathrm{full}} = \frac{|W_{\mathrm{ext}} - E_{\mathrm{total}}|}{E_{\mathrm{total}}} = 3.69\%.
  \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
This $3.69\%$ discrepancy in the post-peak regime is explicitly disclosed. While internal UEL energies satisfy numerical conservation ($|E_{\mathrm{elastic}} + E_{\mathrm{fracture}} - E_{\mathrm{total}}| / E_{\mathrm{total}} < 1.0 \times 10^{-7}$), the small difference between trapezoidal work and internal energy arises from finite incremental dissipation and spatial integration truncation under rapid post-peak softening. It is presented as a characterized numerical limitation, rather than asserted as exact physical identity.

\subsection{Constitutive Storage Units and Extreme History Interpretation}
\label{subsec:hole_history_units}

Extraction of the peak history variable in the severed ligament yielded an extreme value $\Hist_{\max} = 7{,}471.2$. To interpret this physically, an audit of the Fortran UEL source code (\texttt{f42\_mixed\_uel.for}) was conducted. 

In the subroutine, Young's modulus is input as $E_{\mathrm{mod}} = 210.0\,\mathrm{kN/mm^2} = 210.0\,\mathrm{GPa}$. The positive strain energy density \texttt{POS\_M} is evaluated with elastic constants in units of $\mathrm{kN/mm^2}$, meaning that the history variable $\Hist$ is stored in:
\begin{equation}
  [\Hist] = \mathrm{kN/mm^2} = \mathrm{GPa}.
\end{equation}
In fully broken elements ($d \to 1$), the degradation function scales physical stresses to zero ($g(d)\sten_0^+ \to 0$), whereas the kinematic strain tensor $\eten$ continues to increase as crack flanks separate under displacement control. Consequently, the counterfactual undegraded elastic strain energy continues to accumulate monotonically according to the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) history update rule:
\begin{equation}
  \Hist(t) = \max_{s \le t} \psi_0^+(\eten(s)).
  \label{eq:kkt_history}
\end{equation}
The reported value $\Hist_{\max} = 7{,}471.2\,\mathrm{GPa}$ is therefore a purely kinematic strain-energy accumulation within broken elements, not a physical material stress.

\subsection{Terminal Increment Failure Diagnosis}
\label{subsec:hole_terminal_diagnosis}

The simulation stopped at increment 196 of Step~2 ($u_2 = 0.0075785\,\mathrm{mm}$) rather than the nominal input target $u_2 = 0.0120\,\mathrm{mm}$. Inspection of the solver message file (\texttt{.msg}) and status file (\texttt{.sta}) revealed:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Convergence residuals for displacement and phase-field degrees of freedom satisfied equilibrium tolerances throughout all 246 converged increments.
  \item At $u_2 = 0.0075785\,\mathrm{mm}$, the severing of the ligament between notch and hole completed, causing a rapid structural stress redistribution. Monotonic top-edge displacement induced high secondary tensile stresses on the upper-right boundary of the hole.
  \item Secondary damage localization initiated on the hole perimeter, causing the automatic time incrementation algorithm to repeatedly cut back the time increment.
  \item The time increment dropped below the specified solver minimum ($dt < 1.0 \times 10^{-8}$), causing \Abaqus/Standard to terminate with the standard exit message \texttt{"TIME INCREMENT REQUIRED IS LESS THAN MINIMUM SPECIFIED"}.
\end{itemize}
No Fortran UEL numerical crash, floating-point exception, or Jacobian inversion occurred. The termination represents a numerical continuation limit under displacement control at the onset of secondary cracking, rather than an algorithmic UEL failure.

\section{Explicit Scientific Limitations and Boundary of Validity}
\label{sec:hole_limitations}

To maintain rigorous university academic standards, the following scientific limitations are explicitly established for the Method~A off-axis hole demonstration:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single-Mesh Demonstration without Mesh Convergence Proof:} The crack propagation and deflection were evaluated on a single $52{,}447$-element adapted mesh. While local resolution ($h \le 1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$) is substantially improved over the baseline, a formal multi-mesh convergence study on this specific geometry was not conducted due to the December 13 research freeze timeline.
  \item \textbf{Incomplete Spatial Coverage of Resolution Criterion:} While median element sizes in the FPZ satisfy $h \le \ellzero / 5 = 1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, the compliance fractions are $89.4\%$ at the notch tip, $90.6\%$ in the ligament, and $86.3\%$ at the hole boundary. A small fraction of transition elements exceeds $1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.
  \item \textbf{Absence of In-Run State Transfer (Two-Pass Offline Remeshing):} Method~A is an offline pre-refinement methodology. It does not perform dynamic in-analysis remeshing or state transfer during the crack propagation phase. The adapted mesh is solved afresh from the undeformed reference state ($u=0, d=0$).
  \item \textbf{Post-Peak Work-Energy Discrepancy:} While pre-peak energy balance is virtually exact ($0.0018\%$), the $3.69\%$ discrepancy in total work over the full crack-propagation history reflects incremental dissipation and discretization effects during rapid softening.
  \item \textbf{Premature Loading Termination:} The analysis terminates at $u_2 = 0.0075785\,\mathrm{mm}$ upon secondary crack emergence at the hole, leaving the post-hole structural failure unmodeled.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary and Research Conclusions}
\label{sec:hole_summary}

The off-axis hole investigation provides a defensible, source-verified demonstration of indicator-driven adaptive remeshing applied to curvilinear phase-field fracture:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Linear-elastic continuum error indicators (\MISESERI) alone fail to isolate the fracture process zone, leading to element-count explosion ($130{,}435$ elements) without adequate crack-tip resolution ($5.28\% \le 1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$).
  \item A diagnostic pilot simulation ($46{,}747$ elements, Job \texttt{1421048.mmaster02}) revealed the authentic curved physical damage corridor ($d \ge 0.10$).
  \item A three-tier adapted mesh ($52{,}447$ physical elements, $157{,}341$ layered elements) achieved $89.4\%$ to $90.6\%$ compliance with $h \le \ellzero / 5$, improving local resolution by up to $+85.5\%$ while increasing total element count by only $+12.19\%$.
  \item Matched pre-peak parity was demonstrated to within $-0.0264\%$ reaction force agreement and $-0.03\%$ total energy error.
  \item Continuous two-step crack propagation (Job \texttt{1421055.mmaster02}, 246 increments) captured peak load ($F_{\max} = 0.545786\,\mathrm{kN}$), post-peak softening ($25.7\%$ load drop), and smooth curvilinear deflection into the off-axis hole.
  \item Graph-theoretic adjacency analysis conclusively proved continuous topological crack connection to the hole across all damage thresholds from $d \ge 0.50$ to $d \ge 0.95$ ($902$ connected elements at $d \ge 0.95$).
\end{itemize}
These results establish the quantitative capabilities and fundamental boundaries of offline error-guided pre-refinement in phase-field fracture mechanics.
