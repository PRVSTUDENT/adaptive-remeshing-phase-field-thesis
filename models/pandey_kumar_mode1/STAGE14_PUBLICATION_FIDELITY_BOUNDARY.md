## 3. Methodological Circularity & Refinement Heuristic

### 3.1 Two-Pass Offline Pre-Refinement vs. Online Adaptive Remeshing
The framework published by Pandey & Kumar (2025) operates as an **automated 2-pass offline pre-refinement heuristic**, NOT an in-analysis adaptive crack-tracking scheme:
1. **Pass 1 (Pre-Analysis `Job-1_UEL.inp`):** Executes a complete coupled phase-field damage solve on an initial uniform coarse mesh ($h = 0.02\,\text{mm}$).
2. **Mechanism of Corridor Localization:**
   - In the pre-peak elastic regime, the stress error indicator is dominated by boundary bending gradients (~51% far-field error share), which would refine the entire specimen globally (~71k elements).
   - Once the crack initiates and ruptures across the symmetry plane, the separated flanks unload the bulk material.
   - Bulk stress relief extinguishes far-field stress recovery errors ($<10.5\%$), while severe localized strain gradients at the crack face concentrate $86.7\% \to 95.4\%$ of the error integral along the fracture line.
3. **Pass 2 (Production Solve `Job-2_UEL.inp`):** Abaqus `RemeshingRule` ingests this post-rupture error field to generate a stationary refined corridor mesh ($h_{\min} = 0.001\,\text{mm}$) before launching the second, final fracture simulation from $u = 0$.

### 3.2 Methodological Implications for the Master's Thesis
* **Why it functions for Mode-I:** Because Mode-I symmetry enforces a straight horizontal crack path, even an under-resolved coarse mesh captures the macroscopic failure corridor.
* **Limitations:** The heuristic is not a general predictive dynamic remesher for arbitrary or branching crack paths where the initial coarse discretization may fail to predict the true trajectory.
* **Thesis Nomenclature:** The refined candidate mesh is strictly described as an **offline error-guided pre-refined mesh** (`PROJECT_TARGET_LIKE_ADAPTIVE_CANDIDATE`), never as an "online adaptive remeshed solution."
